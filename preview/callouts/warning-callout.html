Overview

When to use a warning callout

Use this callout for important information that:

  • someone needs to be aware of before doing anything else
  • is time critical
  • could have a significant effect on someone's health
  • addresses a common or significant misconception or mistake

When not to use a warning callout

Do not use a warning callout:

  • in transactional pages — NHS England hasn't tested them there yet
  • if the information is not important enough for a warning callout — use inset text or important information

How to use a warning callout

Try to give the callout a short, clearly worded heading, such as 'warning'. Headings help people scan the page for warnings and understand what's relevant to them.

Make the callout concise, specific and self-contained. For example, do not introduce a callout with the words "If this happens...". Explain the circumstances.

Avoid having more than 2 callouts on a page. We want to reassure people, not to scare them.

Keep the warning callout concise. No more than 5 lines on mobile view (this is about 2.5 lines on desktop).

Do not break the flow with a callout. Think about whether you need it and the best place to put it.

Make sure any text below a warning callout has its own heading so that screen reader users know it's not part of the callout.

If possible, put at least 1 short paragraph of text between 2 callouts or between an action link and a callout.

Finish sentences in components with a full stop, unless it's a full sentence link.

Headings

The callout should have a specific heading, for example "School, nursery or work", prefixed with the visually hidden text Important:. This will help users with screen readers understand that the information is important.

If the heading is simply the word "Important" or it includes the word "important", you do not need to use visually hidden text.

Warning callout variants: how to use

Using important information

Use important information for information that:

  • is not time critical
  • someone needs to be aware of before doing anything else
  • would not have a significant effect on someone's health

When not to use important information

Do not use a important information

  • if the information is critical, use the warning callout
  • in transactional pages — NHS England hasn't tested them there yet

Example of important information

Using warning text

Use the warning text component when you need to warn users about something important, such as legal consequences of an action, or lack of action, that they might take.

To use warning text effectively:

  • place it close to the action or decision it relates to so users see it at the right moment
  • state the risk clearly and concisely — say what might happen and why it matters
  • keep the message brief and focused on the key consequence
  • test the placement and wording with users to ensure the warning is noticed and understood

When not to use a warning text

Do not use a warning text when:

  • the information is helpful but not critical — use body text, inset text or a hint instead
  • you're describing routine risks that people already expect or understand
  • the consequence is minor, reversible or low-impact
  • you need to highlight an error — use an error summary or error status instead
  • repeating warnings across the page would dilute their impact or overwhelm the user

Accessibility

Accessibility

WCAG 2.2

The heading and background contrast ratio is 11.92:1 and the content and background contrast ratio is 13.69:1. These exceed the requirements of WCAG 2.2 Success Criterion 1.4.1 Use of Colour, level AAA.
